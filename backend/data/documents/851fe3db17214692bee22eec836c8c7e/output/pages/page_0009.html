The detection limit can be determined in different ways. The simplest approach is based on the signal to noise ratio. The signal to noise ratio is determined by comparing measured signals from samples with known low concentration of analyte with those of blank samples. The concentration showing signal to noise ratio between 3:1 or 2:1 is generally considered as acceptable detection limit. The other approach is based on the standard deviation of the response and the slope. The detection limit may be expressed as: LOD = 3.3 σ/S where, σ = the standard deviation of the response; S = the slope of the calibration curve The slope may be estimated from the calibration curve of the analyte. The σ can be estimated as the standard deviation of the blank. The value of σ can also be estimated based on the calibration curve. For this the specific calibration curve should be studied using sample containing analyte in the range of detection limit.
